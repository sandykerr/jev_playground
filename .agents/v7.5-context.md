## Rationale and recommendations retained from the discussion

- Concentrate essential lifting on three days so intervening days can be easier
  and more flexible. Full-body sessions have different emphases; they need not
  train every muscle heavily each time. Redistribute work rather than simply
  appending all previous upper-body sets to unchanged lower sessions.
- Monday fits predominantly upper-body lifting plus a compact explosive dose
  ahead of pickup. Keep cleans crisp; initially keep pre-pickup leg extensions
  around 2–3 RIR. Chest-supported rows are an option if back fatigue limits
  barbell rows, not an adopted exercise substitution.
- Wednesday gives max velocity and vertical jumping dedicated attention.
  Suggested order: warm-up, max-velocity work, main jump block, then lifting,
  with full recovery for quality field efforts. Exact jump types and doses need
  definition; actual maximal jump practice should have an explicit role.
- Friday has the greatest concentration of demanding lower-body work. Suggested
  order is acceleration, focused COD, then lifting. Start front squats/RDLs
  mostly around 2–3 RIR; avoid making both exhaustive. Session duration and total
  field/gym dose must be evaluated together.
- Leg curls retain knee-flexion hamstring work alongside Friday's RDLs. Avoid
  expanding the remaining exercise list before setting and assessing its volume.
- Pickup contributes athletic exposure but does not establish an adequate dose
  of maximal-velocity running or maximal approach-jump practice. Reassess Monday's
  role once league ends; do not assume pickup continues throughout the macrocycle.
- Full-body organization is a scheduling choice, not an established superior
  hypertrophy method. A [2024 split/full-body meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/)
  found similar strength and hypertrophy outcomes when volume was equated.

## Blaine's effort progression and proposed adaptation

Source details are retained in the [training source register](training-source-context.md).
Relevant transcripts include [hypertrophy phase 2](../Diet_Training_Agent_2026_2027/Transcripts/Training/blaine_mcconnell/2026/blaine_athlete_hypertrophy_phase_2.txt)
and [week 8](../Diet_Training_Agent_2026_2027/Transcripts/Training/blaine_mcconnell/2026/blaine_week_8_hyp.txt).

- Blaine starts around 3 RIR, later uses 1–2 RIR sets plus final AMRAPs, then
  removes AMRAPs for the final block while targeting repeated 1-RIR sets.
  AMRAPs partly calibrate effort estimates. Exercise exceptions include his
  trap-bar strength progression and dumbbell muscle snatches.
- Proposed adaptation: establish the schedule first; progress selected
  hypertrophy sets toward 1–2 RIR. Use occasional final-set AMRAP calibration
  on stable, familiar accessories, replacing a set rather than adding one.
  Stop at the limit of clean repetitions; no forced reps or technique changes.
- Main demanding lower lifts initially stay around 2–3 RIR. Cleans, ballistic
  work, sprints and jumps use execution/output criteria rather than AMRAPs.
  No blanket near-failure rule for calf/rehab work.
- Changing proximity to failure changes workload even if set count is unchanged.
  Do not simultaneously assume more sets are needed. Later-set AMRAP performance
  also reflects rest and accumulated fatigue; it is not a perfect measurement of
  earlier-set RIR.
- An [eight-week trained-adult study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/)
  found similar average quadriceps growth at 1–2 RIR and failure, with greater
  acute fatigue at failure. This supports selective use, not an individual
  optimal-volume claim. These effort recommendations are not yet a weekly wave.
